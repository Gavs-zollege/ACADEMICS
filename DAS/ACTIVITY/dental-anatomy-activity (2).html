Maxillary and Mandibular

The upper jaw and dentition are referred to as "Maxillary".

The lower jaw and dentition are referred to as "Mandibular".

Tips to Remember:

The root word "max" means greatest. If something is the greatest, it is above the rest. The Maxillary is above the Mandibular.

Arches and Quadrants

The upper jaw and dentition of the maxilla bone is referred to as the maxillary arch.

The lower jaw and dentition of the mandible bone is referred to as mandibular arch.

Within each arch there are two quadrants (or fourths) divided by the midline.

Gingivae

The gingivae (gums) are crucial to good dental health.

The gingival tissue helps protect the roots of the teeth and the bone that the roots are connected to.

Periodontics is the branch of dentistry that focuses on the structures surrounding and supporting the teeth.

Tongue

The tongue is a multi-purpose organ made of muscles.

It helps us speak and manage the food we eat. It is covered in thousands of papillae which consist of the sensory organs and nerves for taste and touch.

The membrane that connects the tongue to the floor of the mouth is called the lingual frenum.

Frenula

The Frenula in the mouth are the small folds or ridges of tissue that help provide stability of the upper lip, lower lip, and the tongue.

Palates and Uvula

The hard and soft palates form the roof of the mouth. The hard palate sits at the front of the roof of the mouth. It provides structure in the mouth and allows space for the tongue to move around.

The soft palate does not contain any bone but is a fleshy area that ends in the uvula and it assists speech, breathing, and swallowing.

The uvula is the teardrop shaped soft tissue that hangs down from the soft palate and is visible when a person opens their mouth. The function of the uvula is to block the nasal cavity when a person is eating or drinking.

Anterior and Posterior Teeth

Incisors and canines are the anterior teeth while premolars and molars make up the posterior teeth.

Tips to Remember:

The prefix "post" means behind or after, and starting from the midline, the posterior teeth are after the anterior teeth in each quadrant (from the "front" to the "back" of the mouth).

Teeth Shapes and Functions

Incisors (8):

Your incisors are the four front (anterior) teeth in both the Maxilla and Mandible. These teeth are wedge shape and allow us to bite into food and pull it into our mouths. Think of biting into an apple.

Canines (4):

Your incisors are between 2 canines in both the Maxilla and Mandible. These teeth are also considered anterior and they function about the same as incisors.

Premolars (8) and Molars (12):

These are your back (posterior) teeth. With similar large, squared shapes, the top (occlusal surface) of these teeth are designed to sustain the force used for chewing and grinding. The premolars also help guide food from the canines to the larger molars in back for chewing.
